Privacy Policy

Globeskimmers · Last updated: October 13, 2026

This Privacy Policy explains how Globeskimmers ("we," "us," or "our") collects, uses, and shares information when you use the Globeskimmers mobile application and related services (the "App"). By using the App, you agree to this Policy.

1. Information We Collect

  • Account information. When you create an account or sign in using email, Google, Facebook, or Apple, we collect your name (or first name) and email address to create and secure your account, and to show your first name on guestbook notes you sign and on your shared passport.
  • Home and travel details. You may tell us your home city and country, which personalize the App (for example, which stamps count as a trip). Earlier versions of the App also asked optional questions such as travel budget or accommodation style; if you answered them, those answers stay in your account until you delete it or ask us to remove them.
  • Location data. With your permission, we access your device's approximate or precise location to find places near you (restaurants, ATMs, coffee shops, restrooms, stores, and more) and to provide weather and local information. You may instead enter a location manually, and you can disable location access at any time in your device settings.
  • Stamps and your location. A stamp is earned by being at the place. When you tap Stamp, the App reads your device’s location once and checks it, on your device or (for attractions) on our server, against the place’s own location. Our server also compares the country of your internet connection and how fast you would have traveled since your last stamp, to catch spoofing. For a stamp at a named place, your exact position is used only for that check and is not stored; the stamp keeps the place’s location, the date, and a rough zone of where you stood (for example “on site” or “nearby”, with a distance band such as “under 150 m”), which only our team sees. If the App says you aren’t at a place and you tap “I’m here, but the app is saying I’m not”, you still get the stamp, and we keep a short report so we can fix our map: the place, the date and time, your position rounded to about one city block (only if your birthday shows you are 18 or older), how accurate your phone’s location was, your type of phone and app version, and an anonymous code instead of your name or account. Your reports are deleted when you delete your account. Two kinds of stamp keep a location from your device: a stamp for crossing into a new country, state or region keeps the point where your phone was, and a “Stamp a place” stamp where you don’t pick the city or spot from the search list keeps the location the App was using when you added it, which may be where you are now rather than the place you visited. If you prove a stamp with a photo, the App sends the location and time saved in that photo to our server for the check; they are not stored, and photos are saved without their location tags. Other people never see these coordinates, with one exception: a travel buddy you tag on a stamp receives that stamp’s map point. Otherwise they see only a stamp’s name, city, country and date, plus any photos or note you choose to show, and only for stamps you haven’t hidden.
  • Saved & recent locations. Places you save, and your wishlist, are stored in your account with their locations so they follow you to other devices. Recently used locations are stored on your device.
  • Past partner-link taps. Before October 3, 2026 the App had links to booking partners. Each tap was recorded with a click ID, your account’s internal ID if you were signed in (never your name or email), the partner and product tapped, and the destination city and country. We keep those records for accounting, including after you delete your account.
  • Website messages. If you write to us through the contact form on globeskimmers.io, we store your name, email, company if you give one, which kind of sender you are (traveler, partner, investor, press), your message, the page you sent it from, a hashed version of your IP address (used to limit spam) and your browser’s user-agent string (browser, version and operating system). We use them to reply to you and to prevent spam, and the Globeskimmers team reads these messages in our admin tools. We keep a message until we no longer need it to reply or keep a record, and we delete one on request.
  • Profile & social information. If you use the Virtual Passport's social features, we collect the profile you choose to create: a username (handle), display name, "about me" line, website, favorite places, future destinations, and an optional profile photo. We also collect your date of birth, together with the country your internet connection is in when you enter it (so we can apply local age rules), to apply age-appropriate protections — see Section 9 — and, only in aggregate (never individually), to understand the age range of the people who use GlobeSkimmers. Your birthday is never shown on your profile or passport. If you tag a friend by email, we store that address to deliver the tag; we don’t email it. If you ask us for a username or our verified Seal, we store your request and email.
  • Content you share. The App stores the travel content you create: passport stamps and their notes, memory photos, postcards, signatures, drawings and reactions on friends’ passports, guestbook notes, dish and drink photos you post at a place, private messages you send to a place’s owner, places you suggest as a stamp (the place’s name and location, why you recommend it, and an optional photo) and the likes you give them, and your virtual luggage and travel-buddy items. By default your passport is visible only to you and your friends (people you follow who follow you back); it becomes visible to others only if you make it public or share a page. If your birthday shows you are under 18, only you can see it (see Section 9). You can hide any stamp so that only you see it. Guestbook notes are different: they appear on the place’s guestbook for every App user, under your first name and, if you choose, your home city. Dish photos are shown without your name. Place suggestions are shown to every App user without your name: the place, how many people suggested it, their reasons, the reasons people wrote in their own words, and any photos. If you set a place with “Use my location”, where your phone is at that moment becomes the place’s pin; until we approve the place, other people see that pin only to within about 100 metres. Messages to a place’s owner are delivered to that business once it verifies the place, and carry your first name only if you choose to sign.
  • Usage information. To understand and improve the App, we record how it is used: screens opened, places and cards you tap or save, which maps app you open for directions, time spent on a screen, how long a session lasts, the searches you type, the city and country the App is showing, the kind of device (for example iPhone or Android), and, if you pick one, who you’re traveling with (solo, couple, family or friends). These records are linked to your account and to a random ID created when the App is first opened on a device. We delete them after 180 days and keep only daily totals by city and country. We also keep a list of the random IDs we’ve seen, each for 400 days after we first see it, so we can tell when a new phone opens the App. To stop a script from posing as many new phones, we count first opens from each internet address for the day, keeping only a scrambled form of the address and the date, which we delete after two days. The App shows no advertising and collects no advertising identifiers.
  • App updates. Our update service, Capgo, receives a random device ID, your device platform, the App and operating-system versions, when the App is opened or moved to the background, and whether an update installed or the App closed unexpectedly. It uses these to deliver updates and tell us when something breaks. This is not linked to your account.

2. How We Use Your Information

  • Provide, personalize, and improve the App's features (nearby search, translations, weather, cultural information).
  • Create, authenticate, and maintain your account.
  • Check photos, drawings and text for safety before others can see them, and confirm a stamp from a photo you choose to submit.
  • Check that you live near, or hold a stamp near, a place you suggest, using your home location and your stamps.
  • Tell our team about activity such as a new download, a new sign-up or a new stamp. A notice can include the kind of device, the sign-in method, the country your internet connection is in, a stamp’s place, city and country, and a member number instead of your name; our team can look that number up in our admin tools to see whose account it is. A notice never includes your name or email, never a stamp that is private when it is made, and never a stamp by someone whose birthday shows they are under 18. A report also sends our team a notice that says only what kind of thing it was about and why, never who, with two exceptions: a suggested place sends one only for its first report and for the report that hides it, and reporting a guestbook note itself sends none (a note hidden after three reports is emailed to our team instead). Blocking someone sends a notice that says only that a traveler blocked someone, when the block files a record for our review (Section 8). These notices are capped each day: one person’s reports and blocks send at most 10, their reports about a child’s safety at most 3 more on an allowance of their own, and each kind of notice has its own daily limit, so no kind can use up another’s. Anything past a cap still reaches our team’s review list, without a notice. Our admin tools count stamps by place, city and country without showing who made them, and our team opens one member’s activity only for support, safety or a legal request.
  • Send service-related emails.
  • Maintain security and prevent abuse.

3. How Your Information Is Shared

We do not sell your personal information. We share information only with service providers that help us run the App:

  • Google — Places and Maps (finding places near the location you search from), text-to-speech for language phrases, and Sign-In. Your use of these features is also subject to Google's Privacy Policy.
  • Anthropic — its Claude AI service checks photos, drawings and much of the text you post for safety (see Section 8), recognizes the place in a photo you submit to confirm a stamp, translates phrases, and helps understand what you type in search.
  • Supabase — our database and sign-in provider. It stores your account, home and preference details, date of birth and profile, saved places, stamps and most of the things you post or send, and sends sign-up and sign-in emails. As our sign-in provider it also keeps its own security log of sign-in events, such as signing in, signing out and an account’s deletion, with the email address and the internet address used.
  • Cloudflare — runs our servers and stores your photos, usage records, website messages, and the places you suggest with their likes and reports.
  • Capgo — delivers App updates (see "App updates" above).
  • Open-Meteo and OpenStreetMap (Nominatim) — weather for a location, and place search when you add a stamp for a spot by hand. They receive the location or text needed for that request, not your identity.
  • Maps, images, fonts and flags — some content loads directly from public services: map images from OpenStreetMap and CARTO, fonts from Google Fonts, images from Wikimedia, and flags from flagcdn. Like any website, they see your device’s IP address and the map area or item requested, but not your account. When you ask for directions, the App opens Google Maps, Apple Maps or Waze with your start and end points.
  • ntfy and Twilio — deliver activity notices to our team (see Section 2), with a member number instead of your name.
  • Apple and Facebook — only if you choose to sign in with them.
  • Resend — sends our emails: invitations from our team to people we’ve reserved a username for, letters telling an account it has received our verified Seal, notices to our team about guestbook notes awaiting review, and messages about hotel stays booked in the App before October 3, 2026.
  • Nuitée Travel Ltd — the merchant of record for hotel stays booked in the App before October 3, 2026. For those bookings it holds the guest name, email and stay details it needs to keep the reservation.

We may also disclose information if required by law or to protect rights, safety, and security.

4. Advertising

The App contains no advertising. We do not use advertising SDKs, do not collect or share advertising identifiers, do not track you across other companies’ apps or websites, and do not sell or share your information with data brokers.

5. No Bookings or Affiliate Links

As of October 3, 2026, the App does not sell or book anything and contains no affiliate or partner booking links. Prices shown in the App — for example admission or parking — are there to help you plan, with their source named; to buy, you go to the venue or its official website yourself.

Bookings made before that date. A small number of hotel stays were booked in the App earlier, sold by Nuitée Travel Ltd as merchant of record (Nuitée charged the card and holds the reservation; Globeskimmers never stored card details). We keep the booking record — hotel, stay dates, booking reference, and the guest name, email and phone entered for the stay — so we can help with the stay, cancellations and refunds. It is kept after you delete your account.

6. Data Retention & Deleting Your Account

We retain account information for as long as your account is active. Usage records are deleted after 180 days.

You can delete your account in the App (Settings → Delete Account); globeskimmers.io/delete-account explains how, and you can also email us. Deleting your account removes, automatically: your account and the App’s record of your sign-ins; your home and preference details; your social profile, including your date of birth (with the country recorded with it) and your profile photo; your username, share link and any Seal, and your requests for a username or Seal; your member number; your passport stamps with their notes and photos, and your “I’m here” reports; the travel-buddy tags you sent or received; your follows and blocks, both ways; the postcards you sent or received; the signatures, drawings, reactions and co-signs you left on other passports, and those others left on yours; your dish and drink posts and their photos; your messages to place owners; your virtual luggage and travel-buddy items; your saved places and wishlist; the photos and drawings on your guestbook notes, and your guestbook visit records; and your place suggestions with their photos, and your likes and reports on suggestions. If a step fails, our daily clean-up tries it again until it is done. Accounts deleted before this update kept some of these items; our daily clean-up now looks for what they left and deletes it the same way.

Some information stays. Supabase’s own security log of sign-in events (Section 3) is not part of this deletion. Guestbook tips you posted stay up, shown as “A traveler” without your name, home city or photo. Notes you had deleted stay hidden, with their text kept, also without your name. A guestbook photo or drawing you uploaded but never posted with a note stays in our storage, shown nowhere in the App. An account frozen because its birthday is under 13 is the exception: all its guestbook notes and uploads are deleted (see Section 9). A place that other travelers also suggested stays listed without your suggestion; a place only you suggested is removed unless we had already reviewed it. Usage records stay until they are deleted after 180 days. Any other reports you made about someone’s content, and our team’s record of each block you made, stay as safety records. Some information may also be retained as required by law or for legitimate business purposes, such as the booking and partner-link records described above.

  • Location: grant or revoke location access in your device settings, or use a manually entered location.

8. User Content, Moderation & Safety

  • You and your friends by default. Your Virtual Passport and its photos are visible only to you and your friends unless you make them public or share a page, and any stamp can be hidden so only you see it. If your birthday shows you are under 18, only you can see your passport (Section 9). Your username, display name and profile photo appear next to things you post or send (postcards, tags, comments) and to people you follow or who follow you.
  • Automated review. Photos, drawings and postcard photos are reviewed automatically, using Anthropic’s Claude, before they can be shown to other people; content that fails review is not displayed publicly. Guestbook notes, shared stamp notes, photo captions, comments, dish names, place suggestions and messages to place owners are screened for abuse the same way before others see them.
  • Reporting & blocking. In your Mailbox (a traveler’s postcards, stamps and activity), on Home’s Friends’ travels, in your follow lists, on a shared passport, on a signature or comment on a passport, on a place’s guestbook note and from a tag invite, you can report the traveler and block them. Place suggestions and travelers’ dish and drink photos can be reported in the App, and you can report anything by emailing founder@globeskimmers.io. Reports are reviewed by our team. One person can send at most 30 reports a day in the App; past that, or for anything urgent, email us. A block also tells our team: blocking someone files a record, with what you were looking at when you blocked, for our safety review (no second record is filed for the same person while the first is waiting for our review, even after an unblock, and one person’s blocks file at most 30 records a day; the blocks themselves always work). Blocking is silent (they aren’t told) and works both ways: you stop following each other and neither of you can follow the other (a follow either of you tries anyway is shown only to the one who tried, and reaches no one); pending tag invites between you are declined, and no postcards or tags pass between you; your stamps, postcards and activity stop appearing in each other’s Mailbox; neither of you sees the other’s place guestbook notes; and in the App each of you sees the other’s passport, with its signatures and reactions, as private. Signatures and reactions that someone you blocked left on your passport are hidden from everyone, and someone who blocks you can’t hide from you what they left on your passport. You can see and undo your blocks in Settings → Privacy → Blocked travelers; unblocking doesn’t bring back the follows a block ended. Owners can remove any reaction or comment left on their pages.
  • Removal. To remove content you created, delete it in the App or contact us and we will remove it. A guestbook note you delete comes down at once and its photo or drawing is deleted; its text is kept, hidden.

Sensitive places

You can stamp any place you really visit, including places such as hospitals, clinics, schools, shelters and places of worship. The App never asks why you were there. When we recognize a place like this, from its Google place type or from the name you type, the stamp starts private and visible only to you, and it is left out of our team’s activity notices. You choose whether to share it. Recognition is automatic and can miss a place, and famous landmarks that are also places of worship (for example a cathedral that is an official stamp) count as landmarks. You can hide any stamp yourself. Private stamps are not shown to other users; our team can see them only in admin tools, for support and safety. Anything you write in a stamp’s note is your own content: it is private unless you choose to show it on your shared passport, and we never use stamps or notes for advertising (the App shows no ads).

9. Children's & Teens' Privacy

The App is not directed to children under 13. We ask for your full date of birth to apply the protections below. Once saved, a birthday can’t be changed in the App; to correct it, email us.

Under 13. If the birthday on an account shows that its holder is under 13, we freeze the account at once: its passport is hidden from everyone else, it can’t stamp, post, send or like anything, follow anyone, or save places, a wishlist, luggage or travel-buddy items, and no one can follow it, tag it or send it a postcard: one tried anyway reaches no one, and whoever tried isn’t told. Tag invites it sent before read as expired. Until the clean-up, its guestbook notes and other posts outside its passport stay up without its name, username or profile photo. Our server checks the stored birthdays every day, so this happens whether or not the account is opened again. After 7 days, our daily clean-up checks the birthday again. If it shows the holder was under 13 when the account was frozen, the clean-up deletes the account’s content, even if the holder has turned 13 since. It deletes everything Section 6 lists as removed when you delete your account, except what is kept below. It also deletes every guestbook note the account wrote, including notes it had deleted; every guestbook photo or drawing it uploaded, posted or not; the name, home details and set-up answers (such as travel style, diet and accessibility needs) saved in its App profile; and the App’s record of its sign-ins. It keeps the sign-in account, the age record and any blocks, both ways, so the account stays frozen and an appeal can restore access. The sign-in account keeps its email address; the first name typed at sign-up, or what Google, Apple or Facebook shares, such as a name and a profile-photo link; when it was created and last signed in; and a record of each later sign-in (its date and sign-in method). The age record is the birth year, month and day, when the birthday was entered, the age group, and the country recorded with the birthday. The clean-up also leaves the App’s settings (currency, language, units, display switches and set-up progress), and what Section 6 says stays when an account is deleted, other than guestbook notes and uploads. Its member number is deleted with the rest, but our admin tools can give the kept account a new one. Instead of waiting, the holder can delete the whole account at once from the frozen screen, as described in Section 6; all its guestbook notes and uploads are then deleted too, not left up as “A traveler”.

Appeals. An adult who entered the wrong birthday can appeal by emailing founder@globeskimmers.io (the frozen screen links there). If we accept the appeal, we correct the birthday and the account follows the rules for the corrected age; a corrected birthday that is still under 13 keeps it frozen, and one that was under 13 on the day the account was frozen doesn’t stop the clean-up. An appeal restores access only: content already deleted stays deleted.

Accounts with only a birth year. Earlier versions of the App asked only for the year of birth. If that year shows the holder is under 13 whatever the date, the account is frozen as above. If it could mean 12 or 13, the App asks for the month and day; until it has them, the account can’t stamp, post, send or like anything or follow anyone, and its passport is hidden, but nothing is deleted; the full birthday then decides. For any other year, we count the age as if this year’s birthday hasn’t come yet, until the month and day are added in Settings.

13 to 17. For travelers aged 13–17, social and sharing features are currently switched off, both ways: no following or being followed, no postcards or tags sent or received, and no username, profile, public passport, guestbook notes, dish photos, messages to place owners, signatures or reactions on other passports, or place suggestions. Their account keeps the travel tools and a private passport that only they can see; their stamps never reach anyone’s Mailbox or our team’s activity notices. Guestbook notes, dish photos and passport signatures posted before these features were switched off stay up, without the teen’s name, username or photo, until the teen deletes them; tag invites the teen sent before read as expired. They can like places other travelers suggest (and agree with their reasons) and report a suggestion; a like is stored with their account, deleted with it, and shown to others only as part of a total that never says who gave it. We plan to open social features to this age group later, with safeguards such as friends-only sharing, delayed visibility and approval of new followers, and will update this policy when we do.

Australia. The minimum age for social features in Australia is 16. A traveler aged 13–15 whose birthday was entered over an Australian internet connection is not frozen: they keep the 13–17 rules above — the travel tools and a private passport — even after we open social features to other teens.

If you believe a child under 13 has given us information, email founder@globeskimmers.io and we will delete it.
