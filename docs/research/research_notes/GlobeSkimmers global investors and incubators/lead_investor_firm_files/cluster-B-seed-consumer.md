# Cluster B — Seed / consumer lead candidates (research notes, as of 2026-11-26)

Scope: 17 firms, evaluated as possible LEAD for a ~$1-3M first institutional round into a US consumer mobile travel app (solo first-time founder in LA, live on iOS + Android, ~100 users, pre-revenue, founder employed full time elsewhere).

Method and caveats
- ~33 tool calls: 17 extended web searches + 16 page fetches. The shared web-search budget ran out before a dedicated "consumer traction bar at seed 2025-26" search could run; see the section at the end for what was and was not captured.
- Page fetches that FAILED in this run (so the firm's own words could not be read): Forerunner (`forerunner.com` is an unrelated ministry site; `forerunnerventures.com` safety-check rate-limited; `/approach` 404), Goodwater (`goodwatercap.com` 429 twice), Lerer Hippeau Fund IX Medium post (403), Slow Ventures (`slow.co` rate-limited), Felicis team page (rate-limited). Claims for those firms rest on press and aggregators and are marked accordingly.
- "Aggregator" = Tracxn, PitchBook teaser, Peony, Goldi, Valueadd, Seedlist, Gatekeep, f4.fund, etc. Treat as unverified unless a primary source agrees.
- Fit score = likelihood of a yes to THIS company today. Wave score = how big the splash would be if they led.

## First Round Capital

- Partners covering consumer: the site does not list partners by sector; it names Hayley Barna (Birchbox co-founder — the natural consumer partner), Liz Wessel, Chris Fralic, Todd Jackson, Bill Trenchard in guides/case studies; "Partners often hand companies to the partner with the most relevant experience, so any contact can start the process" ([Source](https://firstround.com/)).

- 2024-26 consumer/travel deals: none captured. Sectors "tend to cluster around enterprise, AI, hardware, healthcare, fintech, and consumer" ([Source](https://firstround.com/)).
- What they say they want (primary): founding team is the biggest factor — resourcefulness, resilience, a track record of decisions; "contrarian insight into how a market works"; a small group of passionate early customers if there is a product; "Customers and revenue aren't required"; "it's never too early to reach out" ([Source](https://firstround.com/)).
- Inbound: no form, but "cold outreach is also accepted, citing a cold email that led to a 2018 investment"; referrals from portfolio founders/angels get a quicker response; process = founder-led first meeting → point partner follow-up → twice-weekly partner meeting → same-day decision; ~half of companies reaching the partner meeting get funded; no NDAs ([Source](https://firstround.com/)). Programs: PMF Method (for founders not yet ready to raise), Angel Track. **PitchAssist is not mentioned on the current site** — earlier descriptions call it a Series A deck/narrative service for portfolio companies, so it is post-investment support, not an inbound door ([Source](https://venturecapitalarchive.com/venture-funds/first-round-capital-firstround-com)).
- Media: First Round Review is one of the most-read founder publications (common knowledge; audience number not captured). Wave 9.
- Location: SF, NYC, Philadelphia; invests nationwide including fully distributed companies; US-focused ([Source](https://firstround.com/)).
- Solo / first-time stance: not stated. The site does say it meets founders "who are still employed elsewhere" and helps with timing and finding co-founders ([Source](https://firstround.com/)) — directly relevant to a founder still in a day job.
- Fit 3 / Wave 9. The language ("never too early", revenue not required, still-employed founders welcome, cold email OK) is the most inviting in the cluster, but the $3.5M average and ~14% target mean a First Round round is bigger than $1-3M and extremely competitive. A cold email costs nothing; a PMF Method application may be the realistic door.

## Consumer traction bar at seed, 2025-26 — what was and was not captured

Not captured: no partner-stated DAU/MAU, retention, or growth threshold from any of these 17 firms was found before the shared search budget ran out. A follow-up search is needed for explicit 2025-26 statements (suggested targets: Forerunner's consumer research, Goodwater Sonar quarterly decks, Lerer Hippeau's Fund IX letter in full, a16z-style "consumer AI benchmarks" posts from any of these firms).

What the primary pages do say, which is the closest proxy:
- First Round: "Customers and revenue aren't required"; wants "a small group of passionate early customers (if there's a product)" and "contrarian insight into how a market works" ([Source](https://firstround.com/)).
- Pear: invests "often before their ideas are fully formed"; most PearX joiners haven't raised yet ([Source](https://pear.vc/pearx/)).
- NFX Harvard FAST 2025 funded pre-seed at $150K-$3M on a SAFE with no traction bar stated ([Source](https://fast.nfx.com/harvard-fast-2025)).
- Goodwater Sonar Q3 2025: "user growth is accelerating" is the metric they chose to headline — a hint that growth rate, not absolute users, is their lens ([Source](https://www.goodwatercap.com/sonar)).
- Maveron: judges "where consumer behavior is changing, often before it's obvious" — a behavior-shift argument can substitute for metrics at seed ([Source](https://www.maveron.com/)).

Implication for a ~100-user, pre-revenue app: lead with insight + the live two-platform product + a retention curve on the users you do have (even 100 users yield a D7/D30 and a stamps-per-active-user number), not with the absolute count. The firms whose own words permit a pre-traction yes are Pear, First Round, and (when a track is open) NFX.

## Cross-cutting observations

1. Pricing spread at the same stage: PearX ~7% for $500K-$2M vs NFX FAST 15% for $1M-$3M vs First Round ~14% target. If the round is $1-3M, PearX is the cheapest institutional money in this cluster.
2. Of the 17, only Pear puts "solo founders welcome" in writing ([Source](https://pear.vc/pearx/)); only First Round puts "still employed elsewhere" in writing ([Source](https://firstround.com/)). Both are directly relevant to this founder.
3. Seven of the 17 (Felicis, Craft, Coatue, Tiger, DST, Goodwater, Forerunner by AUM) are too large to lead a $1-3M round as a matter of fund math, whatever their consumer appetite.
4. Location: none of the 17 is LA-based. First Round states it invests "nationwide, including fully distributed companies" ([Source](https://firstround.com/)); Lerer Hippeau is NYC-centric; PearX requires Bay Area relocation.
5. Two "program" doors in the brief are not what they sound like: First Round's PitchAssist is post-investment Series A support, not an inbound door; Floodgate's Reactor is for students. NFX's generic FAST appears retired (redirect to homepage; no mention there).
