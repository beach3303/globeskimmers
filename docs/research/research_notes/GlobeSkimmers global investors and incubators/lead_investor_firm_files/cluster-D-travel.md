# Cluster D — Travel-specialist and travel-literate lead candidates

Prepared 2026-11-26. Scope: candidate LEAD investors for a ~$1-3M pre-seed/seed into a US consumer mobile travel app (location-verified "virtual passport", ~100 users, pre-revenue, solo first-time founder, Los Angeles).

**Method note.** Searches were short and generic (firm/person names only); no startup-identifying detail was used in any query. The shared web-search budget for this session ran out partway through, so the second half of the research was done by fetching primary pages directly. Anything I could not open or confirm is marked **unverified** — nothing below is inferred from memory and presented as fact. Several primary sources (PhocusWire, eu-startups, BusinessWire, Crunchbase) return 403 to automated fetches; where that happened I say so.

**Scoring key.** *Yes-likelihood* = frank 1-10 chance of a yes to a ~100-user, pre-revenue consumer travel app with one founder. *Wave* = 1-10 size of the signal/amplification if this party led.

**Headline read.** Every dedicated travel fund that is still operating (Thayer, SKY VC, Amadeus Ventures, United Airlines Ventures) has drifted toward B2B / infrastructure / enterprise travel tech, and at least one early-stage program (Antler) is on record as moving *away* from consumer travel-discovery apps because consumer attention is expensive to buy ([Cap Table Insights, 2026](https://captableinsights.com/travel-tech-funding-2026/)). The best-fit *leads* for this profile are therefore not the travel funds but (a) travel-literate generalist seed funds with a consumer-travel precedent in LA/SF (Mucker Capital, Upfront's scout fund, General Catalyst's seed practice, Slow Ventures, 500 Global), and (b) travel-operator angels who still write seed checks (Erik Blachford, Paul English, Hugh Crean, Scott Hintz). The travel funds are better treated as *second-check validators* once there is a lead.

Traction then: **200,000+ travelers in 184 countries**; co-founder Niek Bokkers: "During the trip the concept went viral among fellow travelers"; CEO Koen Droste: "the current online travel experience is utterly broken" ([Polarsteps news, 2017-08-02](https://www.polarsteps.com/news/travel-tracking-app-polarsteps-raises-a-new-900-000-investment)).
- **Jan 2019 Series A, €3M (~$3.5M)** — led by **INKEF Capital**; total raised €4.6M (~$5.25M). Traction then: **1M registered users**, 180+ countries, 52M days traveled, 10 employees; monetization via one-click printed "Travel Books". INKEF's Kyang Yung: "The growth they have been able to achieve with basically zero paid marketing is remarkable, to say the least" ([Polarsteps news, 2019-01-16](https://www.polarsteps.com/news/polarsteps-hits-1-million-users-and-raises-3-million-in-funding); [EU-Startups](https://www.eu-startups.com/2019/01/amsterdam-based-travel-tech-startup-polarsteps-raises-e3-million-and-reaches-one-million-users/)).
- **Since 2019:** no later round found ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/polarsteps/financials) shows $5.08M total). Whether they have raised again or are profitable is **unverified**.
- **Lesson for the comp:** Polarsteps' first institutional lead came at 1M users and a working physical-goods revenue line; its first €1.4M came from media-company money and a local angel pack, not a travel VC.
