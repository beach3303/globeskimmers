# Court Slauson First, Earn Speedrun Later

The single strongest lead investor to pursue right now is **Slauson & Co.**, the Los Angeles pre-seed/seed fund whose published parameters — **$500K–$2M checks, "hoping to lead most rounds,"** a thesis that "your lived experience is your competitive advantage," and a woman-founded travel company (El Camino) already in the portfolio — line up with this round on every axis, with **Wonder Ventures** ("no traction is not a problem," $1–1.5M first checks, Southern California only) run in parallel as co-lead or alternate. Across roughly 300 funds, programs, grants and stages checked on 2026-11-26, the blocker that recurs is not the ~100 members; it is the full-time hospital job — Hustle Fund answers "Do I have to be full time? Yes," YC requires full-time work during and after the batch, Mucker wants "100% committed" founders, and a16z speedrun expects founders to be "building your startup full-time" outside a weekly in-person day. The founder's profile is a strength exactly where the money is smallest and the champions loudest: inclusion-thesis funds (Slauson, Hyphen Capital, Graham & Walker, Gold House, Halogen), Filipino and AAPI doors (Hyphen's "no lead required," Kaya Founders, the Philippine government's new Startup Grant Fund), and the solo-execution story that speedrun, Z Fellows and South Park Commons name in writing. The loudest wave-makers — Alexis Ohanian's 776, Serena Williams's ten-day-old Starfire Ventures, Mark Cuban — have no verifiable application door, so the reachable megaphones are Jason Calacanis's Founder University, Y Combinator's Spring 2027 batch and The Pitch podcast. On the long game: a global tap-to-pay layer is buildable today on partner rails with no licence of her own (Stripe Connect plus Apple Tap to Pay in 59 countries, at 2.7% + 5¢ + $0.10 per tap), but not yet in the Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam or Indonesia, where QR acceptance through Alipay+ or local PSPs is the route; the "biggest travel network" ambition is fundable because every comparable (Booking, Trip.com, Airbnb) was built capital-light; the airline ambition is not — JetBlue needed $128M before its first flight and Breeze more than $300M of equity plus hundreds of millions in aircraft debt. With a16z SR008 declined on 2026-11-26 and YC W27's decision due 2026-12-11, the 60-day plan is a zero-cost stack this week (Cloudflare Tier 3, the Amber Grant by Nov 30, Grid110, Apple featuring), six open investor forms, warm paths into Slauson and Wonder, and a speedrun SR009 re-application with a visible delta once the YC answer lands.

## The hospital job, not 100 users, is the wall every door names

Nearly every institutional door that can write $150K or more asks the same question first, and the answer today is a hard stop. Hustle Fund's FAQ puts it in two words — "Do I have to be full time?" "Yes." — and its founder Elizabeth Yin said in 2021 "I don't know any VC who will fund somebody who is part-time, including ourselves" ([Hustle Fund FAQ](https://www.hustlefund.vc/faq); [Mercury Series Tea](https://mercury.com/blog/series-tea/elizabeth-yin)). Y Combinator says applicants "can certainly apply when you are a full-time student or employee" but must "commit to working full-time on their company during the batch and afterwards if accepted," with the batch in person in San Francisco ([YC FAQ](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)). MuckerLab wants "passionate, 100% committed entrepreneurs" and "fully formed teams of 'builders,'" and adds that "consumer companies need more proof of product-market fit than enterprise or B2B startups" ([Mucker](https://mucker.com/accelerator/)). Harlem Capital requires "at least one full-time founder" alongside $1M–$2.5M checks ([Harlem Capital](https://harlem.capital/pitch/)); Fearless Fund requires the same ([Fearless Fund](https://www.fearless.fund/venture)). Every residency program compounds the problem with geography: PearX says "All teams must relocate to the SF Bay Area for the program" for 12 weeks ([Pear](https://pear.vc/pearx/)); Antler's US residency requires "at least one founder" to be "full-time in residency" for up to three months in SF, NYC or Austin, with no Los Angeles site ([Antler](https://www.antler.co/location/us)); Afore's Founders-in-Residence is a two-month in-person program in San Francisco built for people "leaving or have just left their jobs" ([Afore](https://afore.vc/residence)); South Park Commons runs an eight-week in-person bootcamp ([SPC](https://www.southparkcommons.com/news/f26-founder-fellowship)). The a16z speedrun FAQ is internally inconsistent — one answer says people with full-time jobs need "about one day a week in person" at the SF office, another says the twelve weeks happen in person and "we expect you to be building your startup full-time" ([speedrun FAQ](https://speedrun.a16z.com/faq)). **First Round is the only firm that frames meeting founders "who are still employed elsewhere" as normal**, and even it expects full-time work after a yes ([First Round](https://firstround.com/)). The practical consequence is that every message she sends should pre-answer the question with a dated plan — "full-time on the first $X of committed capital" — because that turns a stated disqualifier into a term.

Solo status is the second, softer wall. YC's FAQ says "Yes. We regularly accept solo founders," immediately qualified by "one-person startups are tough and you're more likely to succeed with a co-founder" ([YC FAQ](https://www.ycombinator.com/faq)); Paul Graham "usually tells YC applicants to get a co-founder" ([Fortune, 2014](https://www.fortune.com/2014/02/25/y-combinators-paul-graham-get-a-co-founder/)); Pasadena Angels states "Two or more founders. Solo founders are typically not considered" and "rarely invest[s] in consumer products" ([Pasadena Angels](https://www.pasadenaangels.com/entrepreneurs)). Carta's data, cited in a snippet the researchers could not open, puts solo-founded startups at 36% of 2024 formations versus 17% in 2017 while noting "VCs remain more skeptical of solo founders than founding teams" ([Carta](https://carta.com/data/linkedin-solo-founder-billion-dollar-company-altman/)). But the written welcomes exist and they are specific: Pear "welcomes solo founders and can help find a co-founder" ([Pear](https://pear.vc/pearx/)); speedrun says "If you're a solo founder with a strong track record of execution, we strongly encourage you to apply," while expecting "a technical cofounder or equivalent expertise" ([speedrun FAQ](https://speedrun.a16z.com/faq)); Graham & Walker lists solo founders as eligible ([Graham & Walker](https://www.grahamwalker.com/pitch)); Z Fellows says "apply solo or with co-founders" and is built for people with full-time jobs ([Z Fellows](https://www.zfellows.com/)); Bloomberg Beta's manual predicts "a one-person billion-dollar company" and says those individuals are "the ones we want to find early" ([Bloomberg Beta Manual](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Bloomberg-Beta/Manual/master/1%20-%20Manual.md)). The fact that neutralizes the objection is the one no investor can dismiss: **a live two-platform product shipped alone in eight months with 2,000+ commits**, which satisfies every stated screening criterion the researchers found — Calacanis's "founders who show up, ship fast, and never stop building" ([Founder University](https://www.founder.university/)), Yin's "scrappiness, or burn rate combined with how much you've been able to achieve with so little" ([Mercury](https://mercury.com/blog/series-tea/elizabeth-yin)), Scott Belsky's "pessimistic enough about execution" ([Belsky](https://www.scottbelsky.com/investing-backup)), Mark Cuban's documented preference for "progress not ideas" ([CNBC, 2017](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/03/23/5-cold-email-tips-from-guy-who-won-over-mark-cuban.html)). It belongs in sentence two of every application.

Traction is the third concern, and here the honest news is mixed. No firm in the sweep publishes a numeric DAU, MAU or retention threshold for consumer seed; the stated bars are qualitative. speedrun: "Traction doesn't have to be a number … momentum of any kind: users coming back, a design partner, a growing waitlist" ([speedrun FAQ](https://speedrun.a16z.com/faq)). CRV's Saar Gur invests "often when a product is only days or weeks old" and insists conviction comes "not from a chart" ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/team/saar-gur)). USV's partners warn that ARR "can mask weak retention" ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/161000-usv-raises-900m-its-biggest-fund-cycle-in-20-years/)); Wonder Ventures says "no traction is not a problem" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/05/pre-seed-investor-wonder-ventures-102m)). The comparables show what the real path looked like: Polarsteps raised €500K from media money in 2016, €900K from Dutch angels at **200,000+ travelers** in 2017, and its first VC round (€3M, INKEF) at **1M registered users** with a printed-travel-book revenue line — INKEF's partner praised growth "with basically zero paid marketing" ([Polarsteps 2019](https://www.polarsteps.com/news/polarsteps-hits-1-million-users-and-raises-3-million-in-funding)); Elude in LA raised a $2.1M seed pre-launch on a ~40,000 waitlist ([TechCrunch, 2021](https://techcrunch.com/2021/08/05/elude-raises-2-1m-to-show-spontaneous-travelers-the-best-destinations-for-their-budgets/)); Thrive led Retro's Series A at roughly 7 million downloads ([ValueAdd VC](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/retro-21-million-series-a-thrive-capital-2026)). The market backdrop is unforgiving: consumer seed funding fell 31% year over year with the median consumer seed round below $1M in Q1 2025 ([Peony, citing Carta](https://www.peony.ink/blog/top-b2b-marketplace-investors)); travel startup funding hit a decade-low deal count in Q1 2026 at about $1B across 44 rounds ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/startups/travel-startup-funding-acquisitions-q1-2026)); Antler's Sarah Finegan is on record moving away from consumer travel-discovery apps because consumer acquisition is expensive ([Cap Table Insights](https://captableinsights.com/travel-tech-funding-2026/)). At ~100 members the absolute count is not the pitch; a D7/D30 retention curve, stamps per active member, and organic acquisition are the numbers that map onto what these investors say they weigh.

Where the profile is a pure strength is in the mandates written for her. Slauson exists "to back founders of innovative technology companies who use their lived experience as their competitive advantage from the earliest stage" ([Slauson](https://www.slauson.co/)); BBG Ventures backs "founders with lived experience" and has a travel-itinerary app in its 52-company portfolio ([BBG](https://www.bbgventures.com/about-us); [BBG companies](https://www.bbgventures.com/companies)); Hyphen Capital invests in "exceptional Asian American founders" with "no lead required" and "the team reads every submission" ([Hyphen](https://hyphencap.com/contact)); Gold House's accelerator was built for Asian Pacific founders and now "all underrepresented communities nationwide" ([Gold House](https://goldhouse.org/futures-entrepreneurship/)); Halogen's portfolio is "Female-led companies: 100%" and it is based in LA ([Halogen](https://halogenvc.com/)); January Ventures selects on "industry expertise, ambition, and tenacity rather than pedigree or network" with 80% female-founded and 38% immigrant founders ([January Ventures](https://www.january.ventures/)). The Filipino heritage opens a second set of doors: Hustle Fund names Southeast Asia as a geography ([Hustle Fund FAQ](https://www.hustlefund.vc/faq)); Iterative announced on 20 Nov 2026 that it will invest outside Southeast Asia with $150K–$500K cheques and run a San Francisco programme from early 2027 ([DealStreetAsia](https://www.dealstreetasia.com/stories/iterative-discontinue-accelerator-programme-501630)); Kaya Founders closed a $25M Fund II "to back more Filipino Founders" from "Day 0" ([InsiderPH](https://insiderph.com/kaya-founders-raises-25m-to-back-filipino-tech-startups)); and DICT's revised Startup Grant Fund, signed 11 Nov 2026 with applications opening 23 Nov 2026, offers Track A of up to ₱500,000 to "individual Filipino founders and unregistered teams" who commit to registering a Philippine business — residency rules are a gap ([Daily Tribune](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/11/17/govt-opens-p15-m-grants-to-unregistered-startups); [Newsbytes.PH](https://newsbytes.ph/?p=113014)). The healthcare background, by contrast, buys narrative only: the J&J and SONSIEL programs fund nursing-led healthcare innovation, not consumer travel ([SONSIEL](https://sonsiel.org/)), and the revenue-gated women's platforms are calendar reminders, not applications — Tory Burch requires "a minimum of $75,000 annually" and closed 17 Nov 2026 ([Tory Burch Foundation](https://www.toryburchfoundation.org/fellows/)); Cartier requires $50K–$5M revenue and 5–250 team members and closed for the 2027 edition on 16 June 2026 ([Cartier](https://www.cartierwomensinitiative.com/regional-awards)); Golden Seeds wants $1M trailing consumer revenue ([Golden Seeds](https://goldenseeds.com/investment-criteria-eligibility)); Astia says "pre-seed companies are too early" ([Astia](https://www.astia.org/faqs)).

## Slauson & Co. fits on all five axes; Wonder Ventures is the parallel bet

Slauson & Co. is the only firm among roughly 75 lead candidates where stage, check size, lead posture, geography and thesis line up without a caveat. Its **$100M Fund II** closed in September 2024 with "checks between $500,000 and $2 million, hoping to lead most rounds," about 30 investments over three years with reserves, and a Fund I record of 38 companies "most led by people of color, at least half led by women"; co-founder Ajay Relan's line is "We invest in everybody" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/04/slauson-co-raises-100m-fund-ii-proving-appetite-for-inclusion-persists/)). The firm invests pre-seed and seed in US companies from Los Angeles, its stated beliefs are "Talent is evenly distributed. Opportunity is not." and "Your lived experience is your competitive advantage," and the portfolio already holds **El Camino, a "travel booking platform for single, female travelers,"** alongside consumer names like ABBY, Livin, Cohart and Pressed Roots ([Slauson](https://www.slauson.co/)). The team includes Austin Clements (founding Chair of PledgeLA, ex-TenOneTen), Relan (brand and culture), Platform Partner Brittany Crockett and Investor Miki Reynolds, who ran Grid110 — which makes Grid110 alumni status a plausible warm path (the Reynolds–Grid110 link is from the researchers' memory and marked unverified) ([Slauson](https://www.slauson.co/)). A stamp passport with official destination partners is a brand-and-culture story, which is Relan's stated lane, and the El Camino holding means consumer travel is not a leap for them — though it also sets up a portfolio-overlap question to pre-empt.

The entry mechanics are the hard part, and the researchers were candid about it: **no pitch form exists** on slauson.co; the only call to action is a newsletter sign-up ([Slauson](https://www.slauson.co/)). The visible on-ramp is the Friends & Family program, and the two public descriptions of it conflict. The homepage still describes a 12-week accelerator in which "20 companies across the country will receive $20,000 in non-dilutive capital" ([Slauson](https://www.slauson.co/)), but the 2026 cohort announcement on 18 March 2026 describes **six consumer companies "selected from over 700 applicants"** receiving **$300,000 each** "at the earliest stage," with inquiries to programs@slauson.co and the instrument not stated ([Slauson Substack](https://slausonandco.substack.com/p/announcing-the-2026-friends-and-family)). No 2027 application link or window is posted; the researchers infer from the March 2026 announcement that a window would plausibly open around year-end or January 2027, which is an inference, not a fact. The honest drags are three: ~100 members is thin even by pre-seed standards, Clements has publicly noted that "as the tides have shifted" the pendulum may swing against inclusion theses ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/04/slauson-co-raises-100m-fund-ii-proving-appetite-for-inclusion-persists/)), and nothing published says how Slauson treats a solo founder or a founder with a day job — that is the first question to ask in the room, and it should be asked with a dated full-time plan already in hand.

Wonder Ventures is the parallel bet because its stage posture is the most explicitly pre-traction of any LA fund. Dustin Rosen raised **$102M in February 2024** — a $57M pre-seed fund plus a $45M opportunity fund — writes checks of **"$1M to $1.5M,"** likes "being the first investor in companies," and said "we're not afraid to jump in before you even build any product" and "no traction is not a problem"; the consumer record includes Honey, Whatnot, Clutter and Tala, and Fund II "has already returned more than twice its investors' capital" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/05/pre-seed-investor-wonder-ventures-102m)). The firm's own site says "Our mission is to invest in Southern California's best founders earlier than anyone else" and "no idea is too early," with 70+ LA founders and executives as LPs and a contact form as the only inbound route ([Wonder Ventures](https://www.wondervc.com/)). Wonder matches four of five parameters (its thesis is generalist rather than inclusion-specific), publishes no stance on solo or employed founders, and one of the two research passes could not load its site at all, so its inbound responsiveness is unverified.

The other brand names fail as leads for reasons the founder should understand rather than fight. CRV's Saar Gur is the one classic seed GP whose own page describes her stage — "often when a product is only days or weeks old," consumer wins DoorDash, Ring, ClassPass, Patreon — and CRV just raised a **$750M fund** for "seed and Series A" naming consumer, but no inbound form exists and she would compete with teams carrying traction ([CRV](https://www.crv.com/team/saar-gur); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/01/venture-firm-crv-raises-750m-downsizing-after-returning-capital-to-investors)). Seven Seven Six verifiably led a **$6M consumer seed (Ticket Wallet, July 2025)** and filed a **$300M Fund IV on 2026-04-20** under Rule 506(c), but its site renders a single sentence with no form, email or policy ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Ohanian); [SEC Form D](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2130737/000123191926000379/xslFormDX01/primary_doc.xml)). First Round accepts cold email and says "it's never too early," but its average initial investment is ~$3.5M with a ~14% ownership target ([First Round](https://firstround.com/)). PearX is the cheapest institutional money found — **$500K–$2M at ~7% ownership**, "solo founders welcome," applications year-round — but requires 12 weeks in the Bay Area; W27 regular applications closed 4 October 2026 and later ones are reviewed rolling, so the summer 2027 cohort (July–October) is the realistic target if leave is possible ([Pear](https://pear.vc/pearx/); [Pear application](https://pear.vc/pearx-application/)). Precursor Ventures has a live direct-submission form that requires a DocSend or PDF deck, and Charles Hudson's July 2026 LP letter says the firm "has always done well with pre-seed and seed-stage entry points and first-time and repeat founders," but check size, current fund and any day-job policy could not be read ([Precursor](https://precursorvc.com/startup.html); [Hudson Substack](https://chudson.substack.com/p/its-all-pre-series-a-now)). Hyphen Capital reads every submission and needs no lead, but its floor check is **$150,000**, so it is a round participant rather than a first ticket ([Hyphen](https://www.hyphencap.com/)).

## Fourteen doors are open this week, and most cost nothing

The zero-cost layer should go out before any investor conversation, because each item becomes a line in the next application. Cloudflare for Startups Tier 3 is **$10,000 in credits for one year** explicitly for "Bootstrapped or self-funded" companies under $1M raised, reviewed in up to 48 hours, covering Workers, KV, D1 and R2 (R2 capped at $2,500), with two catches: "first-time applicants only," and "Effective Dec 1 and for all incoming startups: Workers AI is not covered" ([Cloudflare](https://www.cloudflare.com/forstartups/)). The Amber Grant's Monthly Startup Grant is **$15,000 "for businesses in the idea phase or with minimal sales (under $10,000),"** with a $15 fee, a deadline of 11:59:59 pm Eastern on the last day of each month, one application per email per three months, and three $75,000 year-end grants drawn from monthly winners ([Amber Grant](https://ambergrantsforwomen.com/); [FAQ](https://ambergrantsforwomen.com/faq/)) — file by **30 November 2026** or time it to 31 December after a visible milestone. Verizon Small Business Digital Ready awards **ten $10,000 grants a month through December 2026**, unlocked by completing any two free courses by 7 December, with final decisions by 12 January 2027 and no revenue floor; selection is a weighted drawing ([Verizon](https://digitalready.verizonwireless.com/funding); [details](https://digitalready.verizonwireless.com/funding/details)). Grid110's Launchpad is "free, and we never take equity," ten weeks, virtual, for "LA founders who've already launched," any sector including mobile apps; every program was out of session on 2026-11-26 with no 2027 dates, so the move is to subscribe and email hello@grid110.org ([Grid110](https://grid110.org/programs); [Launchpad](https://grid110.org/launchpad)). Apple's Featuring Nomination is free for any developer account with a minimum three-week lead time and up to three months ahead, scored on UX, design, innovation, accessibility and localization, and the App Store Small Business Program's 15% commission plus free Apple Foundation Models access is a certain approval ([Apple featuring](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-featuring-nominations/nominate-your-app-for-featuring/); [Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/)). Product Hunt launches are self-serve, schedulable 30 days out, and the help center suggests weekends for "smaller teams and side projects," with featuring an editorial decision reserved for a new product or "major update" ([Product Hunt](http://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/17734355-3-set-your-goals-and-pick-your-launch-date); [featuring](http://help.producthunt.com/en/articles/17734341-2-what-kind-of-products-get-featured)).

The payments ambition is more tractable than it sounds, provided the wording is "the trusted payments and acceptance layer for travelers, built on partner rails," not "own the biggest payment system." Apple's Tap to Pay on iPhone requires only integration with a supported payment service provider, which "is responsible for all the necessary certifications," plus an Apple entitlement; the September 2026 Latin America expansion took it to **59 countries and regions**, with Stripe, Adyen and Square among US PSPs and Stripe, Adyen, Fiuu and Grab among Asia-Pacific ones — but the Philippines, Thailand, Vietnam, Indonesia, South Korea and India are **not** on Apple's list ([Apple Tap to Pay](https://developer.apple.com/tap-to-pay/); [regions](https://developer.apple.com/tap-to-pay/regions/); [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/09/22/tap-to-pay-on-iphone-8-more-countries/)). Stripe prices in-person payments at **2.7% + 5¢** domestic, +1.5% for international cards, **$0.10 extra per Tap to Pay authorization**, and a 0.25% Connect fee for platforms that set their own merchant pricing; a Connect platform enabling sub-merchants must sign Apple's Acceptance Platform terms ([Stripe pricing](https://stripe.com/pricing); [Stripe legal](https://stripe.com/legal/apple-acceptance-platform)). In the cash-heavy Southeast Asian destinations the merchant-side route is QR acceptance as an acquiring or merchant partner: Alipay+ markets "one integration for multiple payment apps" to acquirers and claims 150M+ QR merchants and 2B+ consumers ([Alipay+](https://www.alipayplus.com/)); GCash and Maya expose partner-gated APIs reachable through aggregators ([Maya Developers](https://developers.maya.ph/docs/maya-checkout); [HitPay](https://hitpayapp.com/blog/best-payment-gateway-api-philippines)); the national QR schemes and Project Nexus are central-bank membership networks a consumer app cannot "join" as a wallet ([Bank of Thailand](https://www.bot.or.th/en/financial-innovation/digital-finance/digital-payment/cross-border-payment.html); [BIS Nexus](https://www.bis.org/about/bisih/topics/fmis/nexus.htm)). The network ambition and the airline ambition deserve different answers. "Biggest and most trusted travel network" is a fundable 10–15-year narrative because every comparable was capital-light and commission-driven: Priceline bought Booking.com for **$133M** in 2005 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booking_Holdings)); Ctrip IPO'd in 2003 raising **$75M** and later bought Skyscanner for £1.4B ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trip.com_Group)); Airbnb went from a $20,000 YC check and a $600,000 Sequoia seed to a $7.2M Series A ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbnb)). The airline is the opposite: JetBlue needed **$128M of founding capital** before its first flight in February 2000 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JetBlue)); Breeze Airways raised an $83M Series A and a ~$200M Series B — "more than $300 million in startup capital" — before 2022 and a **$228M secured debt facility** for aircraft in November 2026, and its founder David Neeleman had already built Morris Air, WestJet, JetBlue and Azul ([Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2020/10/06/breeze-airways-partnership/); [Skift](https://skift.com/2021/08/20/breeze-airways-raises-200-million-travel-startup-funding-this-week/); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/169993-breeze-airways-raises-228m-in-debt-from-aip-capital-for-new-jets/); [Wikipedia: Neeleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Neeleman)). The researchers found no evidence of any software or consumer-app founder raising for an airline; the honest framing is "biggest travel network by distribution," and if the founder means airline-like supply, "partner airline" or "virtual carrier with a licensed operator" — a route the research did not cover. The 2026 travel market reinforces the point: investors at Phocuswright Europe called funding "still tight and extremely selective," Phocuswright's outlook sees capital "concentrating in fewer companies that show disciplined unit economics, proprietary data and a clear path to scale" with "a shift toward infrastructure and efficiency rather than pure consumer growth plays," and Q3 2026 investors were "weary, not to mention wary, of AI-native pitches" ([PhocusWire](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/startups/ai-divides-travel-funding-mergers-acquisitions-phocuswright-europe-2026); [Phocuswright](https://www.phocuswright.com/Travel-Research/Research-Updates/2026/Travel-Forward-Data-Insights-and-Trends-for-2026); [PhocusWire Q3](https://www.phocuswire.com/news/finance/tracking-travel-startup-funding-rounds-acquisitions-q3)) — these PhocusWire quotes came from search summaries because the article bodies returned 403, so re-read them before quoting in a deck. The implication is to show B2B optionality (verified-visitation data, merchant tools, acceptance) alongside the consumer passport.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the problem. This is not a travel pitch to travel investors — every dedicated travel fund has moved to B2B infrastructure and one early-stage program is on record leaving consumer travel discovery — and it is not a traction pitch, because no firm publishes a number and the comparables raised their first real money at 200,000 to 1,000,000 users. It is a founder pitch to the handful of funds whose mandates were written for exactly this founder, in exactly this city, and whose checks are sized for exactly this round: Slauson first, Wonder beside it, with Hyphen, Graham & Walker and the small-check doors filling the floor. The "wave" the founder wants is downstream of a yes from a form she can actually fill in, not from a celebrity with no door; Calacanis, YC and The Pitch broadcast whether or not they fund, and the travel stages (Global Startup Pitch, Skift IDEA) are the cheapest press per hour she has.

Three moves change the odds more than any application. First, convert the day-job blocker into a term by naming the date she goes full-time against committed capital, because every institutional door asks and none forbids the answer. Second, make the pitch a verified-presence network with B2B optionality — official partner stamps and visitation data for venues, destinations and airlines — because that single reframing turns Plug and Play, Intelak, STB and the 2026 "infrastructure over consumer growth" market from closed to plausible, and it is the same story that makes the payments layer credible later on partner rails. Third, use the Filipino-first audience as a wedge rather than a footnote: it is what makes Hustle Fund's Southeast Asia geography, Iterative's new US-facing checks, Kaya, QBO and the Philippine Startup Grant Fund relevant, and a national stamp series with the Department of Tourism is the one "stampable everywhere" partnership no competitor is positioned to take.
