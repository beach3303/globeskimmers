# Attraction Partnerships & Monetization Playbook — Virtual Passport

**Date:** 2026-09-29 · **Status:** research, no commitments made
**Context:** post-pivot GlobeSkimmers = one product: a Virtual Passport. Travelers collect stamps at real places (top attractions, airports, filming locations), verified by GPS/photo, with memory photos, shared to Instagram/TikTok. This doc answers: how do we get attractions to partner with us, what can we honestly ask for at our size, and where the legal lines are.

---

## Executive summary

1. **There is a proven market for exactly what we sell.** Tourism boards already pay Geocaching HQ ~$5,000 setup + ~$2,500/yr (tiers to $20K) for a GeoTour ([Geocaching forums](https://forums.geocaching.com/GC/index.php?%2Ftopic%2F332662-geotour-costs%2F=), [official page](https://partnerships.geocaching.com/geotours)); 280+ DMOs pay Bandwango for digital passports/trails ([Destinations International](https://destinationsinternational.org/partners/bandwango)); Niantic charged brands up to **$0.50 per visitor** and now sells sponsored PokéStops at **$30/month** to small businesses ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-pokemon-go-i-dev-says-sponsored-locations-can-earn-it-nearly-0-50-per-visitor), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/11/06/niantic-will-soon-let-small-businesses-pay-to-have-a-pokemon-go-pokestop/)).
2. **The credible first ask is not money.** It's: free stamp placement → partner gives a stamp-holder discount (their marginal cost ≈ $0) → we give them a monthly visitor report. Charge nothing until we can show attributable visits.
3. **Affiliate ticketing needs no permission** and is live-able now: Viator pays ~8% (partners 8–12%), GetYourGuide ~8%, Tiqets ~6–8% ([Track360](https://track360.io/blog/viator-getyourguide-affiliate-programs-operator-teardown-2026), [phptravels](https://phptravels.com/blog/how-to-earn-with-the-viator-affiliate-program)). Georgia Aquarium tickets are already sold through Viator/Tripadvisor ([Tripadvisor product page](https://www.tripadvisor.com/AttractionProductReview-g60898-d11478734-Georgia_Aquarium_General_Admission-Atlanta_Georgia.html)).
4. **Disney/Universal licensing is not yet possible for us** — Disney expects proven manufacturing/distribution/track record and historically takes up to ~20% royalties with non-refundable minimum guarantees; the standard advice is "don't approach Disney first" ([Born to License](http://borntolicense.com/news/looking-for-a-disney-license), [SEC-filed Disney license exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1134765/000141588915003009/ex10-1.htm)). Factual, unbranded park stamps (name + date + user photo, no characters/castle art) are the same lane unofficial apps like Touring Plans have occupied for 15+ years.
5. **Olympics: factual venue stamps are fine; Olympic words/marks are not.** The Ted Stevens Act gives USOPC exclusive rights to "Olympic," "Olympiad," the rings etc. **without any confusion requirement** ([Dinsmore](https://www.dinsmore.com/publications/navigating-the-uneven-bars-when-are-companies-allowed-to-use-the-olympic-trademarks/), [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2021/08/03/u-s-olympic-paralympic-committee-gold-standard-trademark-protection/)). An official LA28 path starts at "Official Supplier" tier — still far beyond us; partners pay $50M–$200M ([elev8.la](https://elev8.la/blog/how-to-become-la-2028-olympic-sponsor)).
6. **Our stamp data is a real product at scale, not today.** Attractions/DMOs pay Placer.ai ~$12K–$50K/yr and Zartico ~$10K–$25K/yr for visitation/origin data ([PassBy](https://passby.com/blog/best-placer-ai-alternatives-and-competitors/), [Utah Business on Zartico](https://www.utahbusiness.com/archive/2023/09/13/the-data-that-drives-the-visitor-economy-zartico/)). A free monthly "who stamped you" report is our door-opener; a paid report needs thousands of stamps per market.
7. **The NPS Passport program is the cultural template** — 400+ parks, free rubber stamps, books sold by nonprofit partner Eastern National / America's National Parks, now with virtual stamps in the official NPS app ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport_to_Your_National_Parks), [americasnationalparks.org](https://americasnationalparks.org/passport-to-your-national-parks/)). People already understand "get the stamp at the place." We digitize + socialize that behavior.
8. **Film-trail precedent is tourism boards + studio, not app + studio.** Tourism Ireland/NI built the licensed Game of Thrones doors "passport" and Glass of Thrones trail ([Ireland.com](https://www.ireland.com/en/magazine/ireland-on-screen/game-of-thrones-locations/), [Visit Belfast](https://visitbelfast.com/blog/post/game-of-thrones-filming-locations-belfast-northern-ireland/)); VisitScotland partnered with Sony on Outlander (a £160K campaign returned est. £790K in hotel bookings) ([STV](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/first-ever-guide-to-scotlands-outlander-filming-locations-published)). Our 348 verified film spots stay factual (no stills/logos); the partnership target is the tourism board, which then brings the studio license.
9. **No direct competitor does place-level stamping + partnerships.** been/Visited color maps of countries; Polarsteps tracks trips (18M users, monetizes photo books); Wanderlog/Stippl/Pilot plan trips. Japan's EKITAG (JR East, NFC stamps at 1,200+ stations, stamp-on-photo sharing) is the closest mechanic and proves the loop — but it's Japan-rail-only ([Japan Rail Club](https://www.japanrailclub.com/ekitag-digital-station-stamp/)). The "verified stamp at world attractions + share" niche is open.
10. **First five deals:** Georgia Aquarium (pilot: stamp → discount + report), Zoo Atlanta or an AZA/ASTC cluster, a mid-size DMO trail (Bandwango-style, GeoTour-priced), Northern Ireland Screen/Tourism Ireland film trail, Eastern National alignment (long shot, approach as complement not clone). Details in "First 5 deals."

## 1. How apps get official partnerships with attraction owners — real precedents

### Niantic / Pokémon GO — the per-visit benchmark
- Big-brand model: **cost-per-visit sponsorship, up to ~$0.50 per unique daily visitor** to a sponsored location (initially quoted at $0.15, later corrected upward). McDonald's Japan activated ~3,000 stores this way. Sources: [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/-i-pokemon-go-i-dev-says-sponsored-locations-can-earn-it-nearly-0-50-per-visitor), [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/2017/niantic-charges-pokemon-go-sponsors-up-to-50-cents-per-visitor/), [AdAge](https://adage.com/article/digital/pokemon-s-ad-model-a-cost-visit-basis/304952/).
- SMB model (2019+): **$30/month per sponsored PokéStop** self-serve ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/11/06/niantic-will-soon-let-small-businesses-pay-to-have-a-pokemon-go-pokestop/); program now run under Scopely: [Sponsored Locations for Business](https://explore.scopely.com/en/sponsoredlocations)).
- Tourism model: Safari Zone events with cities/prefectures. Tottori (2017): **89,000 attendees, ~$16M local tourism revenue in 3 days** ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/pokemon-go-travel-tottori-prefecture/)); Yokosuka city co-ran registration through its own site ([Serebii](https://www.serebii.net/pokemongo/reallifeevents/yokosukasafarizone.shtml)). Niantic says tourism boards both approached them and were approached; collaboration = curating routes + events ([UNWTO/Medium](https://medium.com/@unwto/gamification-in-tourism-pok%C3%A9mon-go-and-beyond-2045acea2c1e)).
- **What each side gives/gets:** sponsor pays cash, gets foot traffic + in-game placement; Niantic gives audience + measurement. Lesson for us: the buyer pays for *attributable incremental visits*, priced per visit or flat/month.

### Geocaching HQ GeoTours — tourism boards pay a listing fee
- Official: "flexible pricing for a range of budgets; cost includes marketing, tracking codes, trackables" — no public price ([partnerships.geocaching.com/geotours](https://partnerships.geocaching.com/geotours)).
- Community-reported numbers: **~$5,000 setup + ~$2,500/yr maintenance; packages at $10K and $20K** ([Geocaching forums thread](https://forums.geocaching.com/GC/index.php?%2Ftopic%2F332662-geotour-costs%2F=)).
- Sponsor (usually a DMO/park system) also places and maintains the physical caches. They get: a branded tour page, promotion to the geocaching audience, completion souvenirs, and visit stats. **This is the closest comparable to a "sponsored stamp trail" in our app, and it prices the product: low four figures to start.**

### Untappd — venues pay to be "verified"
- **$899–$1,199/yr per location** (Essentials/Premium); verification exists only via payment; sponsored badges cost extra on top ([Brewlytics pricing breakdown](https://brewlytics.ai/blog/untappd-for-business-pricing), [Untappd help](https://help.untappd.com/hc/en-us/articles/360034389711-Untappd-Account-Types), [Brewer Magazine](https://thebrewermagazine.com/tapped-or-untappd-is-verified-venue-cost-worth-it/)).
- What the venue gets: verified profile, menus, events, engagement with a purchase-intent audience. Lesson: **once stamping drives visible traffic, "verified attraction profile + custom stamp + analytics" is a subscription SKU** (their price point: ~$75–100/mo).

### NPS Passport To Your Parks (Eastern National / America's National Parks)
- Free rubber "cancellation" stamps at ~all 433 NPS units; the nonprofit partner sells the passport books/stickers; retro stickers for missed stamps; virtual stamps + stamp locations now in the official NPS app ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passport_to_Your_National_Parks), [americasnationalparks.org](https://americasnationalparks.org/passport-to-your-national-parks/), [NPS virtual stamp pages](https://www.nps.gov/meve/planyourvisit/virtual-passport-stamp.htm), [NPS app](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=gov.nps.mobileapp&hl=en_US)).
- Economics: the *place* provides the stamp for free (it drives visitation and store traffic); the *program owner* monetizes collectors' merchandise. Lesson: attractions will host a stamp for free if it costs them nothing and flatters them; the money is in the collector, and (for us) in share-driven acquisition.

### CityPASS / Go City — bundled net-rate economics
- Aggregators negotiate **wholesale/net rates** with attractions and sell bundles at ~40–50% consumer savings; attractions accept discounted revenue for volume + marketing ([Singenuity explainer](https://singenuity.com/blog/san-francisco-tour-pass-comparison), [CityPASS Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityPASS)). Go City claims ~60% global pass market share, ~700K passes/yr ([Go City newsroom](https://newsroom.gocity.com/about-go-city)); affiliates earn ~6% ([Travelpayouts](https://travelpayouts.com/blog/go-city-pass-affiliate-program)).
- Lesson: **attractions are structurally used to giving 20–50% off to channels that deliver measurable visitors.** A 10–20% stamp-holder discount is a mild ask by industry standards.

### Yelp / Foursquare check-in deals — the cautionary tale
- Foursquare "Specials" (free/discount on check-in) reached **750,000+ businesses**; Yelp cloned Check-In Offers in 2010 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2010/11/03/following-in-foursquares-footsteps-yelp-rolls-out-check-in-offers/), [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/yelp-targets-foursquare-facebook-checkin-offers-56748)). Foursquare killed gamified check-ins in the 2014 Swarm split ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foursquare_City_Guide)).
- Lesson: check-in-for-discount at *every* business commoditized and died. **Scarcity is the moat: stamps only at famous places (the founder's stamp-worthiness bar), beautiful art, a keepsake page — not a coupon feed.**

### Museums: Smartify — the B2B SaaS precedent
- 700+ institutions (National Gallery, Acropolis Museum, Tower of London) pay **~£1,800–£7,000/yr** tiers for branded digital experiences + analytics ([The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2017/09/22/new-app-invites-art-lovers-to-build-a-playlist-across-museums), [Smartify pricing](https://smartify.org/partners/pricing), [BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/museum-app-with-2m-users-secures-1-5m-backing/)).
- Lesson: museums *do* pay app companies, at £2–7K/yr, when the pitch is visitor engagement + data.

### Reciprocal passports (ASTC / AZA) — "museum passport programs"
- ASTC Travel Passport: free admission at 300+ science centers for members of any participating one (90-mile exclusion) ([ASTC FAQ](https://www.astc.org/membership/find-an-astc-member/passport-faq/)). AZA reciprocity: mostly **50% off admission** across zoos/aquariums ([AZA](https://www.aza.org/reciprocity)).
- Lesson: zoos/aquariums/museums already operate standing cross-discount networks — a "show your stamp, get X% off" mechanic is native to how this sector markets. They are the easiest first partners.

### Tourism-board licensed trails (film tourism)
- Northern Ireland: the Game of Thrones "Journey of Doors" passport (stamps at 10 pubs with carved doors) and the Tourism-Ireland-commissioned Glass of Thrones walking trail — HBO-licensed, tourism-board-driven ([Ireland.com](https://www.ireland.com/en/magazine/ireland-on-screen/game-of-thrones-locations/), [Visit Belfast](https://visitbelfast.com/blog/post/game-of-thrones-filming-locations-belfast-northern-ireland/)).
- Scotland: VisitScotland × Sony Pictures TV "Home of Outlander" — official guidebook of 48 locations; **£160K digital campaign → est. £790K hotel bookings + 8,500 flight searches**; screen tourism worth £161.4M to Scotland ([STV](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/first-ever-guide-to-scotlands-outlander-filming-locations-published), [cinematic-tourism.com](https://cinematic-tourism.com/scotland-outlander-effect-screen-tourism-2025/)).
- Lesson: **the tourism board is the licensee, not the app.** For our filming-location stamps, sell the *trail* to the DMO; the DMO's existing studio relationship covers the IP for any official co-branded version. Unofficial = factual only.

### DMO digital passports (Bandwango) — the direct B2B comp
- Bandwango's Destination Experience Engine powers passports/trails for **280+ DMOs/CVBs**; the DMO sells or gives away the pass and pays Bandwango a license fee (several clients cover the fee from e-commerce margin) ([Destinations International](https://destinationsinternational.org/partners/bandwango), [Bandwango on DMO revenue](https://www.bandwango.com/resources/using-bandwango-to-generate-new-dmo-revenue-streams), e.g. [Visit Seattle Perks Pass](https://visitseattle.org/press/press-releases/perks-pass/)).
- Lesson: DMOs already budget for this exact product. Our differentiators vs Bandwango: consumer-grade app, beautiful collectible art, verification, and viral share pages — Bandwango passes are web-based coupon books with little social gravity.

## 2. What we can realistically ask an attraction for — now vs at scale

**Honest framing:** pre-revenue with a small user base, we have no leverage to charge anyone. What we *do* have: (a) a free, pretty, zero-effort marketing surface; (b) every stamped page shared to IG/TikTok is an organic ad with the attraction's name on it; (c) attribution data no one else gives them for free. The first deals are barter.

### The pitch order (credible → aspirational)

| Order | Ask | Cost to partner | Our size needed | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | **Affiliate tickets** (Viator/GetYourGuide/Tiqets APIs) | none — no permission needed | any | 6–12% commission to us ([Track360](https://track360.io/blog/tours-activities-experiences-affiliate-programs-operator-guide-2026)). Ship first; it also makes every later pitch "we already sell your tickets." |
| 1 | **Stamp-holder discount** (10–20% off next ticket / retail / membership upgrade, via promo code) | marginal only, self-liquidating | any | Industry-normal: CityPASS partners give ~50% off for volume; AZA members give 50% reciprocity. A code also gives *them* redemption tracking — that's the sell. |
| 2 | **Monthly stamp report** (stamps count, visitor origin split, share reach, dwell if we have it) | none | ~1K+ stamps at that venue to be meaningful | Free at first. This is the relationship glue and the future paid product (see §4). |
| 3 | **Licensed stamp art** (their logo/mascot on the stamp; seasonal rotations — Halloween castle, holiday editions) | legal review on their side | after 1–2 quarters of reports | Frame as *their* co-branded stamp with approval rights + takedown clause. Never use their marks before this deal exists. |
| 4 | **Per-referred-visitor fee or sponsored placement** ($0.10–0.50/visit or $50–500/mo flat, Niantic/Untappd benchmarks) | cash | needs proven attributable visits (redeemed codes) | Do not pitch this before we can show a redemption dashboard. GeoTour pricing ($2.5–5K/yr) is the comp for a DMO-level trail. |

### What partners value in return (say this in every pitch)
- **Attributable incremental visits** — the stamp discount code is their proof, not our slideware.
- **Social reach** — stamped pages shared with venue name/geotag; we can report share counts per venue (our share-analytics work supports this).
- **Origin data** — where stamp collectors came from (home market), the #1 thing DMOs buy (§4).
- **Zero ops burden** — GPS/photo verification means no staff training, no hardware, no QR posters (though we can offer an on-site QR as an option).
- **Flattery + control** — approval over their stamp art; being in the "world passport" next to the Louvre and the Grand Canyon is the emotional sell.

### What NOT to ask for yet
- Cash (any form) — before redemption data exists.
- Exclusive category deals — we need density, not exclusivity.
- API/ticketing integrations with their systems — affiliate rails already cover it.

## 3. Licensing reality check

### Disney / Universal — honestly: not yet possible
- Disney licenses through category managers to companies with **proven manufacturing, distribution, financials, and prior licensing track record**; the standard advice is to build a record with smaller IP first and "work your way up" ([Born to License](http://borntolicense.com/news/looking-for-a-disney-license), [inventRight](https://inventright.com/how-i-became-a-disney-licensee/)).
- Terms: royalty on sales **plus a non-refundable minimum guarantee** (Disney's own license template defines the Guarantee as non-refundable minimum royalties; [SEC-filed exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1134765/000141588915003009/ex10-1.htm)). Reported royalty ranges: ~5–10% apparel, 10–12% toys (west, wholesale), and Disney has historically commanded up to ~20% of licensee revenue ([Entertainment Strategy Guy](https://entertainmentstrategyguy.com/2018/09/06/disney-lucasfilm-deal-part-vi-merchandise/), [note.com licensing explainer](https://note.com/keke_ent/n/n29bad3c63849?hl=en)). Industry MGs typically = 25–40% of projected royalties ([AllBusiness](https://www.allbusiness.com/the-minimum-guarantee-a-fundemental-feature-of-licensing-2-8518280-1.html)) — for a Disney digital category this is a five-to-six-figure commitment before selling anything.
- **The realistic path:** (1) today, factual stamps only — "Disneyland Park · Anaheim · [date]" with *generic* art and the user's own photo. No characters, no castle silhouette (the castle logo is registered Disney IP), no park logos or fonts. This is the lane unofficial Disney apps (Touring Plans' Lines, MouseWait) have operated in since ~2009 using factual/crowd-sourced data without a license ([Touring Plans](https://touringplans.com/lines), [MouseWait](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/mousewait-for-disneyland/id331771613)). (2) Build the licensing track record with aquariums, zoos, science centers, city passes, and one tourism board. (3) Approach Disney's digital/experiences category manager only with usage data at their parks + revenue.
- **Risk note:** even factual use must not imply endorsement — no "official," no park imagery in marketing, and a visible "not affiliated" disclaimer on branded-place stamp pages.

### Olympics / LA 2028
- **The law is unusually broad.** The Ted Stevens Olympic and Amateur Sports Act gives USOPC exclusive rights to "Olympic," "Olympiad," "Citius Altius Fortius," the rings, "Team USA," etc., **with no likelihood-of-confusion requirement** — confirmed by the Supreme Court (SFAA v. USOC) ([Dinsmore](https://www.dinsmore.com/publications/navigating-the-uneven-bars-when-are-companies-allowed-to-use-the-olympic-trademarks/), [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2021/08/03/u-s-olympic-paralympic-committee-gold-standard-trademark-protection/), [Sterne Kessler](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/where-what-and-how-trademarks-the-olympic-games/)). USOPC actively ramps enforcement before each Games ([Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/usopc-ramping-up-trademark-enforcement-as-paris-summer-olympics-approach)). Rule 40 restricts Games-time association marketing even without marks ([Knobbe](https://www.knobbe.com/updates/olympics-advertising-dont-be-ambushed-rule-40/)); "indirect ambush" (imagery/color schemes implying the Games) is the gray zone that gets companies letters ([INTA](https://www.inta.org/perspectives/features/the-risk-for-brand-owners-of-ambush-marketing-during-major-sporting-events/)).
- **What we CAN do:** factual venue stamps. "Crypto.com Arena · Los Angeles · July 2028 · watching gymnastics" is a factual record of the user's visit — venue names used to refer to the actual place are classic nominative fair use, same as Touring Plans naming Disney rides. Note LA28 itself calls it "**DTLA Arena**" in Games materials because Crypto.com hasn't bought Olympic naming rights ([LA28 venue page](https://la28.org/en/games-plan/venues/dtla-arena.html), [The Stadium Business](https://www.thestadiumbusiness.com/2025/08/14/la28-to-display-naming-rights-for-olympic-venues/)) — we use the real-world name, not LA28's.
- **The line:** never use in the app, stamp art, notifications, or marketing: "Olympic(s)," "Olympiad," "LA28," "Team USA," rings, torch, medal iconography, or a stamp *collection* themed as "Games 2028." A "Summer in LA 2028" city collection of venue/attraction stamps is fine; anything that curates *Olympic venues as Olympic venues* invites a letter, because Ted Stevens doesn't require confusion. No Games-time push campaigns that reference the event.
- **Official path (for completeness):** LA28 Founding Partners ≈ $200M, Official Partners $50–150M, Official Suppliers "lower entry" but still major, licensees negotiate royalties + MGs directly with LA28/USOPC — no public application, months of negotiation ([elev8.la](https://elev8.la/blog/how-to-become-la-2028-olympic-sponsor), [ACC Docket](https://docket.acc.com/node/3971), [LA28 licensees](https://hospitality.la28.org/en/partners)). **Verdict: not our game in 2026–28.** Ride the traffic wave with factual LA venue/attraction stamps instead.

### Filming locations (our 348-spot dataset)
- Facts ("scenes from X were filmed here") aren't copyrightable; what's protected is the film's expression: stills, clips, logos, stylized title fonts, character likenesses. Keep stamp art original (the place, not the film), name films in plain text, and we're in the same lane as every guidebook ([Finding the Universe GoT guide](https://www.findingtheuniverse.com/game-of-thrones-ireland-filming-locations/) is a working example of unlicensed factual film-location content). Official co-branded film trails come *through tourism boards* that hold the studio relationship (§1).

## 4. Data as the product

**What buyers pay today:**
- **Placer.ai** (foot traffic, trade areas, visitor origin): custom annual subscriptions, typically **$12K–$50K/yr**, enterprise from ~$50K ([PassBy comparison](https://passby.com/blog/best-placer-ai-alternatives-and-competitors/), [SoftwareFinder](https://softwarefinder.com/analytics-software/placer-ai)). Attractions and retail both buy it.
- **Zartico** (DMO "destination OS": geolocation + card spend): DMOs pay tens of thousands — documented examples: **Sanford NC $25K/yr; Marfa and Alpine TX $10K each** ([Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/archive/2023/09/13/the-data-that-drives-the-visitor-economy-zartico/)).
- **Arrivalist** (visitor origin/arrivals, campaign attribution; acquired by AirDNA 2023): flat fee or CPM scaled to media plans ([Virginia Tourism partner page](https://vatc.org/marketing/advertising/partneradvertising/arrivalist/), [Arrivalist](https://www.arrivalist.com/)).
- What they buy: **visitor origin markets, visitation trends, dwell, cross-visitation, campaign attribution** — exactly the fields a stamp event generates (home region, timestamp, venue, verified presence, plus share reach as a bonus no panel has).

**Could our stamp data sell?** Yes, with three honest caveats:
1. **Scale:** a DMO report needs thousands of stamp events per market per month to beat Placer's panel. Pre-scale, our data is a *relationship gift* (free monthly per-venue report), not a product.
2. **Bias:** our users skew tourist/collector — that's actually a *feature* for DMOs (they care about visitors, not locals), and worth stating in the pitch ("100% verified visitors, 0% locals-noise").
3. **Privacy:** opt-in, aggregated, k-anonymized (no report cell under ~20 users), and the privacy policy must disclose aggregate licensing before we sell anything. Never sell row-level location.

**Product ladder:** free per-venue monthly PDF → paid "city pulse" report to DMOs at **$5–15K/yr** (undercutting Zartico's small-market price) → API tier later. Share-reach per venue ("your attraction appeared in N Instagram stories via our stamps") is a metric none of Placer/Zartico/Arrivalist can offer.

## 5. Competitors — and how to outflank them

| App | What it is | Social | Monetization | Partnerships | Exploitable weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **been** | Country/state checklist map | none to speak of | Pro IAP ($2.99 iOS / $9.99 Android) ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=visited.countries.tracker.been.places.map&hl=en_US)) | none found | Country-level only; no places, no verification, no keepsake. |

## First 5 deals to chase

1. **Georgia Aquarium (Atlanta)** — the founder just visited; largest US aquarium, ~2.4–2.9M visitors/yr, nonprofit ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_Aquarium)). Already deep in discount channels: Atlanta CityPASS anchor ([CityPASS](https://www.citypass.com/atlanta/georgia-aquarium)), $20 Tuesdays, community access ([georgiaaquarium.org](https://www.georgiaaquarium.org/booking/20-tuesdays/)), and sold via Viator/Tripadvisor ([product page](https://www.tripadvisor.com/AttractionProductReview-g60898-d11478734-Georgia_Aquarium_General_Admission-Atlanta_Georgia.html)) — so affiliate revenue works day one with no deal. **Pitch (to their marketing/partnerships team):** free custom whale-shark stamp with their art approval; stamp holders get a promo code (their choice: % off next visit, retail, or membership upgrade — memberships are their stated priority); we send a monthly report: stamps, visitor origin, IG/TikTok share reach. Ask for nothing but a promo code and art sign-off. **Caution:** their FAQ bars unauthorized ticket resale/promotional use ([FAQ](https://www.georgiaaquarium.org/frequently-asked-questions/)) — get written sign-off before any GA-branded stamp or promo; until then the stamp stays factual/unbranded.
2. **Zoo Atlanta + 2–3 AZA/ASTC neighbors (an "Atlanta Culture Trail")** — zoos/museums already run 50%-off reciprocity ([AZA](https://www.aza.org/reciprocity), [ASTC](https://www.astc.org/membership/find-an-astc-member/passport-faq/)), so cross-discount mechanics need no education. **Pitch:** a 5-stop stamped trail; complete it → each venue's chosen perk. Gives us density in one city (the founder's city advantage) and a case study with N venues instead of 1.
3. **A mid-size DMO (Discover Atlanta first; or any CVB that already bought Bandwango/GeoTours)** — they demonstrably pay $2.5–25K/yr for passports and data (GeoTour ~$5K setup ([forums](https://forums.geocaching.com/GC/index.php?%2Ftopic%2F332662-geotour-costs%2F=)); Zartico $10–25K ([Utah Business](https://www.utahbusiness.com/archive/2023/09/13/the-data-that-drives-the-visitor-economy-zartico/))). **Pitch now:** free official city stamp trail + free quarterly origin report, in exchange for placement in their newsletters/site. **Pitch at scale:** $3–5K/season sponsored trail (GeoTour-priced), report upsell.
4. **Northern Ireland Screen / Tourism Ireland (or VisitScotland)** — they already fund licensed film trails and measure ROI in bookings (£160K → £790K precedent, [STV](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/first-ever-guide-to-scotlands-outlander-filming-locations-published)). We arrive with 348 verified film spots already live. **Pitch:** we power the *mobile, verified, shareable* version of the GoT doors passport; start unpaid/cross-promo, structured so the board's HBO/Sony relationship can later bless official art. This is our wedge into studio IP without ever pitching a studio cold.
5. **Eastern National / America's National Parks** — long shot, flagged as such: they own the passport brand and the NPS app already has virtual stamps ([americasnationalparks.org](https://americasnationalparks.org/passport-to-your-national-parks/)). **Pitch:** complement, not clone — we drive their book/retro-sticker sales from our park stamp pages (affiliate/retail referral), they let us note "official cancellation available inside." Never imitate their cancellation stamp design. Even a soft alliance neutralizes the "why not just use the NPS passport?" objection.

**Explicit long shots (don't spend founder time yet):** Disney/Universal character or castle art (§3 — build the track record first), any Olympic-marked content (§3 — factual venue stamps only), selling data before ~10K stamps/month in a market (§4).
