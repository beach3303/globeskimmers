# Business Insurance Research — GlobeSkimmers (solo CA founder, travel app), Oct 2026

## 1. Which policy answers which risk

### Risk 1 — IP claims over landmark stamp art (trademark / trade dress by big rightsholders)
- The realistic home for this risk is **media liability**, usually sold as part of (or an endorsement to) **Tech E&O**. Media liability covers defense + damages for **defamation, copyright infringement, trademark/trade-dress infringement, and misappropriation of likeness arising from content you publish** — exactly the stamp-art scenario. Sources: [Vouch — media liability](https://www.vouch.us/coverages/media-liability), [Liberty Specialty Markets Tech/Media E&O](https://www.libertyspecialtymarkets.com/ch-en/product/technology-media-errors-omissions-insurance), [Corgi media liability](https://www.corgi.insure/media-liability).
- **Patent is excluded essentially everywhere**; tech E&O/media policies cover copyright and trademark but not patent ([Foundershield Tech E&O](https://foundershield.com/coverage/tech-eo)). Stamp art is a copyright/trademark/trade-dress exposure, not patent, so this exclusion doesn't bite here.
- Plain **general liability (CGL)** is NOT a substitute: its "advertising injury" clause is routinely stripped of IP coverage by endorsement, and insurers bar it entirely when you buy separate media liability ([Farella Braun + Martel on CGL + IP](https://www.fbm.com/tyler-c-gerking/publications/cgl-coverage-for-false-advertising-and-intellectual-property-claims-sometimes-its-there-but-you-need-to-know-where-to-look-for-it/), [IRMI e-business policies](https://www.irmi.com/articles/expert-commentary/media-liability-coverage-in-tech/media/ebusiness-policies)).
- **Dedicated IP-defense insurance exists but is impractical at this scale.** IP defense cost reimbursement insurance (defense costs only, no damages) runs **~1–2% of the limit: $10k–20k/yr for a $1M limit** ([IRMI definition](https://irmi.com/term/insurance-definitions/intellectual-property-defense-cost-reimbursement-insurance), [TCB Magazine on IPISC](https://tcbmag.com/patented-coverage/)). CFC sells a broader startup/SME IP policy (defense + damages + enforcement) but prices only by quote ([CFC IP for startups](https://www.cfc.com/en-us/resources/articles/2023/03/intellectual-property-insurance-for-start-ups-and-smes/)). Context: a trademark suit with <$1M at stake costs ~$327k to defend — which is why media liability inside Tech E&O is the only affordable route.
- The risk is real, not theoretical: the Hollywood Sign, Empire State Building, Chrysler Building, Yankee Stadium etc. are trademarked, and building owners have sent demand letters over illustrations on merchandise ([Selling-Stock: Can landmarks be trademarked?](https://www.selling-stock.com/Article/can-landmarks-be-trademarked), [UIC Review of IP Law](https://repository.law.uic.edu/ripl/vol15/iss4/2)).

### Risk 2 — User-generated content (guestbook notes/doodles, dish photos, private owner feedback)
- **Section 230** is the first-line defense: the platform is generally not treated as publisher of user content, including for defamation/trade-libel by reviewed businesses ([Freedom Forum on §230](https://www.freedomforum.org/what-is-section-230/)). But §230 is an affirmative defense you still pay lawyers to raise — insurance pays those defense costs.
- **Media liability does not automatically cover UGC.** Base forms often cover only content *you* create; platforms need an **"expanded definition of media content" endorsement** covering hosted third-party content, and insurers want the business model disclosed up front ([dig-in: social media coverage limits](https://www.dig-in.com/opinion/insuring-your-online-presence-limitations-on-social-media-coverage), [Business Insurance: insurers shun UGC](https://www.businessinsurance.com/insurers-shun-user-generated-content-news-media/), [Corgi: content-heavy platforms](https://www.corgi.insure/blog/what-insurance-provider-handles-media-liability-for-content-heavy-saas-and-ai-platforms)). Screening/moderating content (GlobeSkimmers screens the private feedback) is an underwriting positive, but ask the broker to confirm in writing that moderated UGC is inside the definition of covered "media content."
- **FTC Consumer Reviews Rule (effective Oct 2024, enforcement started Dec 2025–2026):** suppressing or gating negative reviews while displaying positives is an unfair/deceptive practice; penalties up to **$53,088 per violation**; the FTC sent its first warning letters Dec 22, 2025 ([Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/blogs/consumer-products-and-retail-navigator/2026/01/ftc-warning-letters-over-consumer-review-rule), [KJK enforcement note](https://kjk.com/2026/08/06/ftc-consumer-review-rule-enforcement-what-businesses-need-to-know/)). **Insurance will not pay FTC advertising-practice fines** — fines/penalties are broadly uninsurable except narrow cyber privacy-regulatory carve-outs ([IRMI: regulatory defense & penalties](https://irmi.com/term/insurance-definitions/regulatory-defense-and-penalties-coverage)). The kind-notes-public / negative-private design is a sentiment-based display question for a lawyer, not an insurable one. (Note: the rule mainly targets a business curating *its own* reviews; a neutral platform routing private feedback is a different posture — but get a legal read, because no policy covers this.)

### Risk 3 — Ordinary app risks (GPS/photo data, later payments, kids 13+)
- **Cyber liability** covers breach response, liability for leaked personal data (location + photos qualify), and **regulatory defense and penalties** for privacy regulators — one of the few coverages that can pay fines ([IRMI](https://irmi.com/term/insurance-definitions/regulatory-defense-and-penalties-coverage)). COPPA matters: the amended COPPA Rule took effect June 23, 2025 (broader "personal information," stricter retention/consent), and TikTok's **$400M COPPA settlement** has carriers scrutinizing children's-data sub-limits ([Insurance Business Mag](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/cyber/tiktoks-400m-settlement-is-pushing-companies-to-recheck-cyber-and-dando-sublimits-587102.aspx)). With users 13+ and an age gate, expect underwriting questions about children's data; answer them precisely — the age gate helps.
- **General liability** is nearly irrelevant for an app-only business but is cheap (~$180/yr startup median via Vouch) and often contractually required (office lease, events, some partner agreements) ([Quotesweep 2026 cost guide](https://www.quotesweep.com/blog/tech-startup-insurance-cost)).
- **Umbrella** sits on top of GL/auto only — it does NOT extend E&O, media, or cyber limits, so it's not the tool for IP/UGC risk. Buy higher E&O/cyber limits instead.
- Payments later: cyber policies add PCI assessment coverage; flag it when payments go live.

## 3. Exclusions and traps to watch

- **Prior-knowledge exclusion (the big one here).** These are claims-made policies; claims arising from circumstances the insured knew pre-inception that a reasonable person would expect to produce a claim are excluded, and courts enforce this strictly — a 6th Cir. case killed coverage for exactly a known trademark dispute ([Wiley: prior knowledge fatal to trademark E&O claim](https://wiley.law/newsletter-869), [Shumaker client alert](https://www.shumaker.com/insight/client-alert-it-ends-with-us-having-no-coverage-what-corporate-insureds-need-to-know-about-prior-knowledge-exclusions-in-their-liability-policies/), [Anderson Kill](https://andersonkill.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/PublicationID_1646_Prior-Knowledge-Exclusions-in-E-and-O-Insurance-Policies.pdf)). **What this means for the founder:** knowing that some landmark designs are protected and *holding those stamps back* is a risk control, not a known claim — disclose the practice on the application ("we review stamp art for protected designs and withhold matches"). Never hide it: an undisclosed known circumstance can void coverage entirely; a disclosed control usually improves the quote. If a specific rightsholder has already sent any demand letter, that specific matter will be excluded — insure *before* any demand arrives.
- **UGC scope:** confirm hosted third-party content is inside "media content" (endorsement if needed) — see Risk 2 sources above.
- **Patent:** excluded in all of these; irrelevant to this app today.
- **Intentional/willful acts:** willful infringement is excluded; the hold-back practice is also the defense against that exclusion being invoked.
- **Fines/penalties:** uninsurable except cyber privacy-regulatory sublimits; FTC review-practice penalties are not covered — manage by design + counsel.
- **IP-stripped GL:** don't rely on "advertising injury" in a GL/BOP; assume it's endorsed away.

## 4. What $20–100/mo realistically buys

At ~$240–1,200/yr: a **Hiscox-style tech E&O with cyber included at $1M limits plus cheap GL** fits near the top of that range (PL from $22.50/mo + GL from $30/mo), and marketplace quotes for a pre-revenue solo LLC often land at $600–1,200/yr for a $1M E&O/cyber bundle. It will NOT buy standalone IP-defense insurance ($10k–20k/yr), $2M limits across the board, or guaranteed-broad UGC wording — those need ~$3k–7k/yr (the Vouch/Embroker tier). The honest play on this budget: $1M bundled tech E&O + cyber with a media-liability component, written answers from the broker on (a) UGC inside media content and (b) the stamp hold-back disclosure, and step up to $2M + Vouch/Embroker when revenue or an app-store/partner contract demands it.
